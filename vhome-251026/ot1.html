Malaysia OT Rules — Quick Guide

Plain-English guide for common monthly-rated employee calculations under the Employment Act 1955 where the relevant provisions apply.

1. Normal working day

OT is generally paid at no less than 1.5 times the hourly rate for qualifying hours beyond normal daily working hours.

2. Rest day

For monthly-rated employees, work up to half the normal daily hours generally attracts an additional half-day ORP. Work over half up to normal hours generally attracts one additional ORP. Excess hours are generally paid at no less than 2 times the hourly rate.

3. Paid public holiday

Work on a qualifying paid public holiday generally attracts two days' wages at ORP in addition to the holiday pay entitlement. Extra hours beyond normal hours are generally paid at no less than 3 times the hourly rate.

4. Ordinary rate of pay

For a monthly-rated employee, statutory ORP is generally monthly wages divided by 26. The hourly rate is generally ORP divided by normal daily working hours.

Before using this for actual payroll

  • Confirm whether the employee is covered by the applicable statutory OT and rest-day provisions.
  • Use the legally applicable normal working hours and the correct weekly rest day.
  • Check whether the holiday is a gazetted paid public holiday, a substituted holiday, or another type of day off.
  • Check the employee's wage components, shift arrangements, contract terms and any applicable collective agreement.
  • Check the applicable rules in Sabah or Sarawak separately where relevant.
  • Working-hour limits and OT limits are separate from the calculation of OT pay. This calculator does not determine legal compliance with those limits.
Official reference: Employment Act 1955 — official JTKSM PDF. Relevant provisions commonly include sections 60A, 60 and 60D, together with section 60I and the First Schedule.
